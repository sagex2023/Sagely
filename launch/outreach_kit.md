# Outreach kit (no calls, no cold DMs)

Goal: grow the free list using content you already make. Budget: about 2 hours a week.

## 0. Rules that apply to every channel

1. Only post picks that are in the published issue. Never change a pick after the fact.
2. Start with the mathematical worst case, not the upside.
3. Keep the disclaimer in the footer or reply: "Educational only, not investment advice. Options involve risk."
4. Never call a result "guaranteed," "risk-free," or "can't lose." A collar's floor is after costs and has caveats. Never call income from Entry Optimizer or Rent Check protection.
5. Don't use your employer's name, logo, or non-public knowledge. Don't post about tickers on your exclude list.
6. Read each community's self-promotion rules before you post. If unsure, share something useful with no link and let your profile do the work.

## 1. Auto-generated posts (X, LinkedIn, Threads)

After every run, the pipeline writes `out/social-YYYY-MM-DD.txt` with three ready posts: a Risk Limiter worst-case post, an income post (Entry Optimizer or Rent Check, with what you give up), and a weekly track-record post. You review and paste, about 5 minutes. Use a scheduler (Buffer free tier or LinkedIn's scheduler) so it is one batch a week.

Profile line: "Former Marine. I share plain-dollar options ideas for stock owners: get paid to wait, get paid to hold, or set a floor. Worst case first, every pick logged."

## 2. Newsletter recommendations and swaps (highest-leverage channel)

Subject: Swap recommendations?

> Hi [Name], I read [Newsletter] every week, especially [specific issue or idea]. I write The Sagely Report, a weekly email that shows stock owners how to get paid to wait, get paid to hold, or set a floor. Each idea states the worst case first, and every pick is in a public track record. Our readers overlap on [topic]. Would you like to recommend each other? I can send a sample issue first.
>
> [Your name]

Targets: small and mid-size newsletters on options, income investing, retirement planning, dividends, and veteran finance. Aim for lists of 1,000 to 20,000 readers. They reply. Send 5 a week.

## 3. Evergreen articles (search traffic compounds)

Post one a month on the site or Buttondown archive. Draft titles:

1. Get paid to wait: what a cash-secured put really risks
2. Rent on your shares: what a covered call gives up
3. What a collar really costs: floor vs ceiling on [ticker] today
4. Why "zero-cost collar" does not mean zero risk
5. Income funds, buffer funds, or doing it yourself: an honest comparison
6. How we filter options: open interest, spreads and conservative fills
7. A year of logged picks: what worked and what did not (after 12 months of data)

Each ends with the signup form. Use the pipeline's real numbers only after you confirm your data provider's rules on sharing data.

## 4. Communities (value first)

Reddit options, investing and retirement communities, Stocktwits, and veteran-finance groups. Pattern: answer questions with a clear example (like the landing page's), with no link in the first post. Add a link only where the rules allow it.

## 5. Retirement-minded and veteran audiences

Guest posts on retirement-income and veteran-finance blogs and podcasts ("former Marine explains getting paid to wait for a stock"). Keep it educational. Keep your employer out of it.

## 6. Lead magnet (phase 2)

"Risk Limiter Reality Check": a one-page worksheet or small calculator that shows the floor and the cap for any stock the reader types in. It works well because it is useful by itself. Build it after the first 100 subscribers.
