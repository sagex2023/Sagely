## Email 2 - day 2: "Entry Optimizer in 3 minutes"

Subject: Get paid to wait for your price

> Pick a stock you would be glad to own and a lower price you would be glad to pay. You are paid up front to agree to buy it there. If the stock stays above that price, you keep the cash and buy nothing. If it falls below, you buy at that price, minus the cash you were paid.
>
> And you can do it again. If the stock stays above your price, you keep the cash and can get paid again next cycle. If you end up buying, you switch to Rent Check. It is a process, not a one-off trade.
>
> The catch: if the stock falls below your price, you buy it at that price, and every $1 lower costs you another $100 on 100 shares. At zero, you could lose almost the full price. This is a cushion, not a floor. And the pay next cycle will change with the market. Example: [landing page link].

## Email 3 - day 5: "Rent Check in 3 minutes"

Subject: Get paid while you own it

> Own 100 shares? You can be paid rent to agree to sell them at a higher price. The rent is yours to keep, and it softens a small drop.
>
> Collect the rent again and again. Every cycle you keep the shares, you can be paid again. That is why we see this as a process, not a single trade. Our landing page walks through four cycles.
>
> The catch: your gains stop at that higher price, the rent changes each cycle, and it does not protect you from a big fall.

## Email 4 - day 9: "Risk Limiter and what it costs"

Subject: Choose your worst case, and see what it costs

> A collar sets a floor under your shares and a ceiling above them. You pick the worst case. The upside you give up helps pay for the floor.
>
> Protection has a price. A tighter floor means a lower ceiling, so you give up more of the upside. Pick the trade on purpose. A floor at zero or better after costs is rare, and we label it when it happens. It is still not risk-free.
