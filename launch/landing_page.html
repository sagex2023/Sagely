Three jobs for options on stocks you own or want to own

Each email picks setups that fit the market today and shows what you get and what you give up. The first two earn income. Only the third sets a floor. We never blur that.

1 · Getting in

Entry Optimizer

Get paid to wait for your price.

You get
Cash now for agreeing to buy a stock at a lower price. If it stays above that price, you keep the cash. If it falls to it, you buy at a discount.
You give up
Big rallies while you wait. If the stock falls hard, you still own it. This is a cushion, not a floor.
If it falls below your price
You buy 100 shares at your price (our example below: $92) and keep the cash you were paid. Every $1 lower costs you another $100. If it went to zero, the mathematical worst case is almost the full price. You then own the stock and can switch to Rent Check.
Again and again
Stock stayed above your price? Keep the cash and get paid again next cycle. If you end up buying, switch to Rent Check.
2 · Holding

Rent Check

Get paid while you own it.

You get
Rent on shares you already own. It lowers your cost and softens a small drop.
You give up
Gains above the sell price you agreed to. The rent cushions a small drop. It does not protect you from a big fall.
Again and again
Collect rent every cycle you keep the shares, as long as the market pays for it. The amount changes each cycle.
3 · Protecting

Risk Limiter

Choose your worst case.

You get
A floor under your shares at the price you pick. The upside you give up helps pay for it.
You give up
Gains above the ceiling, plus a small cost.

Collect the rent again and again

This is not a one-off trade. Each cycle you keep the shares, you can sell another call and get paid again. Each cycle you keep waiting, you can sell another put and get paid again. Here is Rent Check over four 90-day cycles on a $100 stock, with $223 of rent each time. In real life the amount changes every cycle.

If the stock drifts up, with a dip: you collect $892 of rent. In cycle 4 the stock ends at $112, so your shares are sold at $108. You make +$800 on the shares plus $892 of rent, or +$1,692. Just owning would have made +$1,200. Then you can go back to Entry Optimizer and get paid to wait for a lower price.

Floors, labelled honestly

A floor at zero or better is rare, because the market charges for it. When one exists, we flag it. We keep it separate from floors that rely on dividends being paid.

See the three kinds of floor
Capital-protectedAt expiry, the worst case is zero or better after costs, ignoring dividends. Rare. It only holds if you keep the shares to expiry.
Protected only if dividends are paidThe floor clears zero only because of expected dividends. Dividends can be cut, so we say so.
Near-protectedA small, known worst-case loss. Most collars land here.

We never call anything risk-free, because nothing is.

Common questions

Is this investment advice?

No. This is general education sent to every subscriber. It does not look at your goals, money or risk tolerance, and the author is not your financial advisor.

Do I have to own the stock?

For Rent Check and Risk Limiter, yes: you hold 100 shares per contract. For Entry Optimizer you hold cash instead, enough to buy 100 shares at the price you agreed to. Each idea says which one applies.

Can a collar still lose money?

Yes. Most collars have a small known worst-case loss. Even a floor at zero is measured at expiry, only if you keep the shares until then, and before costs like commissions and taxes. You also give up gains above the ceiling,. If the stock ends above the ceiling, your shares can be sold at that price, which can trigger taxes. Early assignment can also change the result.

What could go wrong with Entry Optimizer and Rent Check?

Entry Optimizer: if the stock falls hard, you still end up owning it at the agreed price, so you can lose a lot, less the cash you were paid. Rent Check: the rent only cushions a small fall, and your gains stop at the agreed price. Neither sets a floor.

How will I see your track record?

On a public page that we update with each issue. It lists every idea we publish, winners and losers, how many have settled, and the typical, best and worst results. The page goes live with the first issue. Until then there is no history to show, and we will not make any up.

What if you get something wrong?

We fix it and say so. If a number in an issue is wrong, the next issue opens with the correction and what changed, and the track-record page is corrected too. We do not quietly edit past results.

Where do the prices come from?

Delayed market data from a commercial provider, run through a fixed set of filters. Each email names its data source and filters.

Do you trade these ideas?

Each email has a position disclosure. Positions are never traded ahead of publication.

Why should I trust you?
